## Multi-rotor designs and executable limits

Two complete models now replace the earlier open-ended RX2 design note:

- **RX2-O:** two arbitrary wirings with odometer stepping, n=1…4, explicit stepping and turnover phases, both directions and 16 contact-alphabet pairs: **16,640 labeled structural settings**.
- **RX2-D:** two independent regular clocks over n1,n2=1…4, with phases/directions explicit, removing constant-relative-angle schedules: **6,080 labeled settings**.

The design supplies forward/inverse equations, a worked toy example, error-model boundaries and an exact modular feasibility reduction. Equal-output equations form components with fixed differences; injectivity leaves forbidden differences between component offsets. A complete assignment gives two extendible partial permutations. The checker reports SAT, UNSAT or UNKNOWN, never converting a budget limit into an exclusion.

Initial toy checks passed 9,416 exhaustive cases; a separate internal challenge added 131,072 exhaustive q=4 cases and 200 q=6 oracle comparisons, including composite-modulus and gauge checks. No K4 input was used. These tests validate the mathematics, not an English recovery solver.

The review corrected a scope trap: constant-relative-angle rotors collapse to one effective rotor, but **192 labeled nonzero-boundary-phase single-rotor settings remain outside R87's tested schedule**. They stay OPEN. A stationary second rotor can also supply an arbitrary output substitution and must not be falsely discarded as R87-equivalent.

The design sets finite development, fresh power-screen, full-method gate and eventual blind-run caps. A blind RX2-O solver must first demonstrate recovery on hidden fresh controls. A separate exact audit is bounded at 22,720 labeled settings × 201 inputs, 10,000 branch assignments per call and 20 million over the entire job, with a 1,800-second wall/CPU cap and 2,048 MB aggregate RSS. Any UNKNOWN result prevents a whole-scope exclusion. Single-error relaxations require separately registered call counts and budgets.
