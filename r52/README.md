# R52 — Crib-conditioned K4 plaintext guesses: a negative control, NOT a decryption

**Research date:** 30 October 2026 (America/Chicago). **Status:** Exploratory, already observed before this GitHub write; **not preregistered or independent blind evidence**. Original ciphertext unchanged. No contact with any external validator or retrieval of Sanborn's unpublished archival K4 plaintext.

## What we actually know, versus hypothesis

The 97-letter original K4 has two confirmed plaintext blocks: zero-based positions **21–33 = EASTNORTHEAST** and **63–73 = BERLINCLOCK**. The fixed layout is exactly `21 unknown + 13 confirmed + 29 unknown + 11 confirmed + 23 unknown`; **73 unknown letters** remain. Sanborn's 2025 public statements associate K4 with a 1986 trip to Egypt, the 1989 Berlin Wall collapse, the World Clock and *delivering a message*. He has described K4 as an instruction; this does **not** establish any of those themes occur verbatim or that the World Clock generates its key.

References: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/cia-kryptos-puzzle-creator-releases-final-clues/ and https://www.wired.com/story/jim-sanborn-auctions-kryptos-key/

Eight **original, human-authored** 97-letter plaintext proposals in `educated_plaintext_probe.py` were constructed to meet the published block locations, not generated by deciphering K4. Never import them into `strictblind/blind` or use them as training labels, positive plaintext fixtures, or candidate-reveal ground truth.

## Three intelligible example hypotheses (nothing confirmed outside the two bold blocks)

1. **Navigation / message delivery:** "The path to follow points **east northeast**. Follow the line until you reach the **Berlin Clock** and look for a new beginning."
2. **Second physical layer:** "Look from the wall toward **east northeast**. The last message was placed beside **Berlin Clock**. The second layer is waiting."
3. **Narrative / Egypt / Wall:** "The Cairo messenger went **east northeast**. The message from Egypt reached the **Berlin Clock**. Witness the fall of the wall."

All three are exactly 97 A–Z letters once spaces/punctuation are stripped. The editorial punctuation is invented, not a claim about K4 encoding.

## Real exploratory test (not a claimed cryptanalytic breakthrough)

- For each of eight candidate plaintexts compute a 97-position implied key `k[i] = CA^-1(CT[i]) - PA^-1(P[i]) mod26` with `PA/CA` among **AZ/AZ, KRY/KRY, KRY/AZ, AZ/KRY**. **This key ALWAYS exists for any candidate**; its existence and exact forward re-encryption have **zero evidentiary value** by themselves.
- Test whether any of 24 short lags (1–24) shows an unusual number of equal adjacent-in-lag key symbols; optimize over 4 alphabet combinations × 24 lags. Compare with **2,000 unknown-position shuffles per candidate**, preserving all 24 confirmed letters and full original candidate unigrams.
- Adjust again for selecting the strongest from the **eight manually authored** candidates: use 2,000 null iterations, each shuffling all eight and taking the best of all 8×4×24 searches.
- Positive test: encrypt one original candidate **independently** under a random period-9 AZ Vigenere key. A correct detector should pick lag 9 and strongly separate it from 300 shuffled nulls.

### Actual local outcomes (20–30 October 2026 exploration; GitHub CI reruns all numerics)

| Case | Best lag | Maximum Z | Uncorrected candidate-specific empirical p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Route and handoff | 19 | 2.355 | 0.835 |
| Instruction and hidden object | 19 | 2.944 | 0.432 |
| Wall and clock delivery | 16 | 2.822 | 0.534 |
| Another-agent handoff | 16 | 2.822 | 0.539 |
| **Physical second layer** | **7** | **4.132** | **0.043** |
| Nonliteral clock-key interpretation | 13 | 2.706 | 0.589 |
| Egypt to clock | 3 | 3.424 | 0.173 |
| Cairo and fall of Wall | 7 | 2.488 | 0.710 |

**Crucial familywise correction: p ≈ 0.289855 (579 of 2,000 null trials at or above observed maximum).** The strongest apparent "period 7" vanishes on accounting for our eight choices; no credible key signal. Repeating-key positive control picked true **period 9** and beat all 300 nulls (empirical p≈0.003322). This detector has power for simple repeating keys, NOT for unknown transpositions/masks or more complex ciphers.

**Scientific conclusion:** These guesses are linguistically reasonable exercises but currently cannot be distinguished from thousands of other English strings by any independent decrypting algorithm. The public 24-letter mask is not itself a plaintext-recovery engine. Do not label any text "possible solution" without a fixed, externally motivated key mechanism that generalizes to 20 fresh plants and exactly forward-encrypts the raw 97-letter source.

## Next more productive experiment

**Unknown-key recovery**, not another million English sentence templates. Build a generative candidate model that explicitly jointly infers (1) language/word segmentation, (2) an independently restricted key family and (3) one or two substitution alphabets or a small route. Benchmark on 20 fresh 97-letter messages with key unknown, freeze blind outputs and explanations before public clue checking. The R44b known-key 20/20 success is a capability but not proof that an unknown key can be identified.

If the proposed mechanism has enough free parameters to decode *any* sentence, then it has zero discriminatory power. Measure false-positive candidate fluency against null ciphertexts using the **same search budget and selection rule**.

This round doesn't involve World Clock city names and does **not** justify claiming the clock is merely a rabbit hole, either as key or as message content. It is **explicitly exploratory**. No Git merges to main authorized.
